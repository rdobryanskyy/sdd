# Probing frames — worked examples

These are six internal lenses for Phase 2. They are lenses for *you*, not labels for the user.
Never say "now I will do a premortem". Each frame below gives:

- what the frame finds,
- a **flat** question (the question of a neutral interviewer),
- the **framed** question (stronger, it makes the user take a position).

Mix the frames. Do not use the list in sequence.

Each framed question also obeys the hard rules. Ask it through AskUserQuestion with 2-4
concrete options, and mark the first option `(Recommended)`.

---

## 1. Premortem — "it's six months later and this failed. Why?"

This frame finds the failure mode that the user does not want to look at.

- **Flat:** "What are the risks?"
- **Framed:** "Six months passed, and you stopped this project silently. What is the most probable cause?"
  - *(Recommended)* You had no energy left before the results grew. Your speed as one person is the real constraint
  - The audience did not come. There was no demand
  - It worked, but it was not worth the time that it used
- **Shows:** whether the threat is motivation, demand or opportunity cost. Each threat has a very different fix.

## 2. Second-order — "it succeeds. What breaks next door?"

This frame finds the cost of success that the user did not include in the plan.

- **Flat:** "What occurs if it works?"
- **Framed:** "It is a success, and your inbound requests double. What fails first?"
  - *(Recommended)* Your calendar. You cannot serve the demand that you make
  - Quality. You cannot keep the standard that you set in week 1
  - Focus. It takes your time from the work that gives you money
- **Shows:** success has a cost. If you name it now, you can change the scale that you aim for.

## 3. Naive listener — "how does this sound to someone hearing it cold?"

This frame finds insider words and assumptions that have no support.

- **Flat:** "Is the message clear?"
- **Framed:** "An intelligent person from outside your group hears the one-line description. What do they understand incorrectly first?"
  - *(Recommended)* They think that it is for beginners, but you mean seniors
  - They cannot tell what they will *do* differently after they read it
  - They think that it is a sales funnel and stop their attention
- **Shows:** the difference between what you mean and what the listener understands. Usually, this is a problem with the audience or the promise.

## 4. Inversion — "what if you did the exact opposite?"

This frame finds whether the selected shape is a real choice or only a default.

- **Flat:** "Is this the correct format?"
- **Framed:** "Instead of long-and-thorough, what if it was short-and-frequent?"
  - *(Recommended)* Short-and-frequent. Here, momentum is more important than depth
  - Long-and-rare. Depth is the only differentiator, so keep it
  - A mix. A short cadence, with one deep item each month
- **Shows:** the user defends or abandons their default. In the two cases, the choice becomes conscious.

## 5. Cost of waiting — "what changes if this slips a quarter?"

This frame finds false urgency and also real urgency.

- **Flat:** "When do you want to launch?"
- **Framed:** "If you start this in three months and not now, what do you really lose?"
  - *(Recommended)* Nothing structural. Thus, decrease the risk first, and start smaller
  - A window closes. A trend or an audience moment goes, and you cannot get it back
  - Compound growth. Each month of delay is a month of audience that you do not build
- **Shows:** whether "now" is a real constraint or only anxiety that looks like a deadline.

## 6. The other person — "what would they say if asked this?"

This frame finds an idea for a different person, where the user answers for that person.

- **Flat:** "Will your team accept it?"
- **Framed:** "Your most skeptical teammate is in the room. Which objection do they give first?"
  - *(Recommended)* "We tried this and it died" — there is a history that you do not give enough weight
  - "Who owns it when you're out?" — the bus-factor question
  - "This competes with X we already do" — an overlap that you did not name
- **Shows:** objections that the user is too close to see. The interview goes through the person who really decides.

---

## Picking a frame

- If the idea is **vague** → use naive listener (get a clear one-line description) + inversion (is the shape a choice or a default).
- If the idea is **ambitious** → use premortem + second-order (find the cost of failure and the cost of success).
- If the idea is **urgent** → use cost of waiting (test the deadline).
- If the idea is **for a team / a different person** → use the other person (go through that person).

If a lens gives a question that you cannot change into 2-4 concrete options, the question is not
ready. Pick a different lens. Do not ask the question as an open question.
