# Annotated pass — a medium-depth interview, start to finish

This is a full example in a realistic shape. Use it to calibrate the shape and the speed of your
questions. The idea is ordinary on purpose. The notes in `> [why]` tell the reason for each step.
There are eight questions in total, which is medium depth.

**User's opening line:** "I want to start a weekly newsletter for engineering managers."

---

### Depth

> [why] Ask one question, then keep the answer. Do not interview about the interview.

**Q0 — How hard must I test the idea?**
- **(Recommended) Medium — the standard pass (6-10 questions)** ← picked
- Easy — a quick check (3-4)
- Hard — question each assumption (10-15)

"Medium depth. Start with the idea in one sentence. You already gave it: a weekly
newsletter for engineering managers."

---

### Phase 1 — Understand the idea

> [why] The one-line description exists. Thus, do not ask "what is it". Go directly to the
> precision of the audience, because "engineering managers" is a very large group.

**Q1 — Which manager, exactly?**
- **(Recommended) New EMs in their first year** — the strongest pain, the least help ← picked
- Mid-level EMs (3-5 years) — the largest population
- Directors / EMs-of-EMs — the highest leverage, the smallest audience

> [why] They selected new EMs. This makes all of the next steps more precise. Now fix the outcome.

**Q2 — What does a reader DO differently after a good issue?**
- **(Recommended) Tries one concrete tactic that week** — a change in behavior, not only a feeling ← picked
- Feels less alone in the role
- Saves it to read "later" (be honest if this is the real outcome)

> [why] "Tries one tactic" is a strong outcome that you can test. Go to the next phase. Phase 1 used 2 questions.

---

### Phase 2 — Stress-test (the core)

> [why] Use cost-of-waiting first. "Weekly" is the most important word, and the speed of one
> person usually stops a project of this type.

**Q3 — Weekly is a promise. What occurs in the first week that you miss?**
- **(Recommended) Start bi-weekly — keep a sequence that you can really hold** ← picked
- Keep weekly — the cadence IS the product, so make a buffer of 4 issues
- Weekly, but permit weeks with no issue, and tell this at the start

> [why] They changed to bi-weekly. This decreases a real risk. Now do a premortem on the demand side.

**Q4 — Six months passed, and you stopped. What is the most probable cause?**
- **(Recommended) Each issue took 5 hours to write, not 1** — the effort became too large ← picked
- Nobody subscribed
- It worked, but it was not worth the time

> [why] Their fear is the effort, not the demand. Ask more about it, because it is a new assumption.

**Q5 (drill) — Where do those 5 hours go?**
- **(Recommended) Research about what to write** — there is no repeatable source ← picked
- Writing and editing of the prose
- Design / formatting / sending

> [why] The root cause is the *source of material*, not the writing. This changes the full idea.
> Tell the user now, not at the summary.

"The weakest spot is real. Without a repeatable source of material, week 3 is an empty page.
Solve this before you call it a newsletter."

> [why] Do a naive-listener check on the positioning before you go out of Phase 2.

**Q6 — A new EM sees the title for the first time. What do they think?**
- **(Recommended) That it is general leadership text with no value** — it needs a hook specific to EMs ← picked
- That it is too junior for them
- That it is a funnel for a paid course

---

### Phase 3 — Propose new angles

> [why] Now propose new angles actively. The problem with the source of material points to a better shape.

**Q7 — The source of material is the real constraint. Change the shape to fix it:**
- **(Recommended) "One real EM situation, dissected" each issue** — your own week is the source ← picked
- Curated links with commentary — the lowest effort, the lowest differentiation
- Interview a different EM each issue — high value, high coordination cost

> [why] The selected angle removes the 5-hour problem. The source becomes the week of the
> user, which they already have. Stop here.

---

### Final summary (full — 8 questions)

## Revised idea
A **bi-weekly** letter for **first-year engineering managers**. Each issue **examines one real
situation from the week of the author** and ends with **one tactic that the reader can try**.
The cadence is sustainable, the source is part of the format, and the promise is about
behavior, not inspiration.

## What surfaced
- **Hidden assumptions**: that "weekly" had no cost, and that the writing was the difficult part.
- **Main tradeoff**: reach (all "EMs") vs precision (first-year EMs). The user selected precision.
- **Weakest spot**: a repeatable source of material. The solution: use the week of the author as the source.

## Alternative angles
1. One real situation examined in each issue (selected — it removes the problem with the source).
2. Curated links with commentary (the lowest effort, the weakest differentiation).
3. An interview with a different EM in each issue (the highest value, but it adds the coordination that the author wanted to avoid).

## Next step
Write three "one situation dissected" issues from the last three weeks *before* you
announce anything. Prove that the source is real, then decide the cadence.
